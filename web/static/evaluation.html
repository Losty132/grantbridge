    How these numbers are worked out

    Answer key: your team's decisions. Pursue and Maybe mean "good fit"; Pass means "poor fit" unless the reason is about capacity, timing or money (those are left out). A relevance mark, if set, always wins. Grants that have closed or that your survey now filters out are left out.

    Fair grading: accuracy is measured on the ranking from your strategic plan alone. The learning box then shows what changes when the app also learns from your decisions; a grant is never moved by its own decision.

    Score by decision: the average plan-based score of grants you pursued versus grants you passed on as a poor fit. Top-5 accuracy: of the first 5 on your list that you judged, how many were good (needs 3). Average position: where your good grants sit on the list. Ranking agreement: each good grant compared with each poor-fit one; how often the good one is higher.

    Reach: a grant counts as "likely missed by manual monitoring" when it comes from a federal agency outside your usual partners AND was found only by searches beyond your agency's own keywords (someone watching either would have caught the rest). Time saved is an estimate: grants reviewed × 3 minutes to skim one by hand.

    Colours: green is good, amber worth a look, red needs attention. Grey means not enough decisions yet; nothing is guessed.

    A pilot prototype that helps North Carolina agencies find, screen and prioritize federal grants. People make every decision; nothing is ever submitted automatically.
